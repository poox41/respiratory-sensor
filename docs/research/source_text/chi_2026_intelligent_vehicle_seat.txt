1 Introduction
Driven by the global revolution in artificial intelligence and
sensing technologies, the adoption of intelligent systems has
emergedasapivotaldirectioninthetechnologicaltransformation
of the automotive industry [1]. The advancement of intelligent
driving technologies is gradually shifting the primary driving

toapronouncedreductioninoverallaccidentrates[ 2].Asacore

2.5 TriboelectricSensingArrayforDriving
PostureMonitoringandAlerting
With the rapid development of intelligent vehicles, in-vehicle
sensorsplayacriticalroleinmonitoringfatiguedriving,environ-
mentalperception,andbecomeacoredrivingforceinautomotive
intelligenttransformation[ 41].Toenablereal-timemonitoringof
driverposture,atriboelectricsensingarrayconnectedtoamulti-
channel Bluetooth device was integrated into three locations of
the car seat: the headrest, left side, and right side. This formed
awirelessmonitoringsystemforidentifyingthedrivingposture
(Figure6a).Whenthedriverisfatigued,theheadsubconsciously
tiltssideways.Firstly,wesimulatedtheresponseoftheheadrest-
based sensing interface to the driver’s head-turning motions
(Figure 6b; Figure S26a). When the head was in the normal
position, the sensors did not produce any noticeable step-like
signals associated with head-turning motions. When the driver
simulatedaleftwardheadturnandreturn,thesensorproduced
a clear downward step-like voltage signal, with the duration of
the head tilt corresponding to the peak-hold time. Similarly, a
rightward head turn generated a comparable voltage signal, but
withthepeakdirectionreversed(Figure S26b).
The electrical signals generated by the sensing interface can be
transmitted via Bluetooth to the in-vehicle intelligent terminal,
where they are analyzed to determine the driver’s posture
(Figure 6c). When the driver maintains an upright posture, the
headrest sensing interface records signals from two units, while
allsensingunitsontheleftandrightsidesrecordvoltagesignals
corresponding to pressure from the back. When the driver is
fatigued, actions such as head lowering and leaning forward
occur, making it difficult for the headrest interface to detect
signals, and partial units of the left and right interfaces record
electrical responses. The real-time monitored signals produced
aheatmapcorrespondingtodriverposture,wheredarkercolors
indicate stronger electrical signals (Figure6d). The heatmap
clearlyrevealsthecontactpointsbetweenthedriverandtheseat,
allowingforanaccurateassessmentofdriverposture.
To further enhance signal recognition accuracy, the headrest
sensor data were trained using a convolutional neural network
(CNN)-baseddeeplearningapproach(Figure 6e).Ofthedataset,
70% was used for training and 30% for testing the model’s
accuracy.Electricalsignalscorrespondingtoscenariosincluding
nodriver,suddenbraking,leftwardglance,normalposture,and
rightward glance were simulated for analysis. With increasing
training iterations, the recognition accuracy of the electrical
signals significantly improved (Figure S27). Figure 6f shows
the confusion matrix of classification results, where each row
represents test samples in the actual class, and each column
represents predicted classes. The results indicate that the CNN-
based model achieved an average recognition accuracy of 98%
for headrest movements. To better simulate the influence of
vibrationnoiseonthesensingsignalduringvehicleoperation,we
collectedadatasetofsensingsignalswithsuperimposedvibration
noise. The results show that the model maintains a recognition
accuracy of over 90% for sensing signals under vibration noise
(Figure S28). In summary, with the continuous advancement
of intelligent driving systems, the triboelectric sensing array
holdsbroadpotentialforapplicationsinassisteddriving,remote
warnings,andothersafety-relatedfields.

4.2 PreparationofAramidNanofibers(ANF)
Kevlar fibers (10 g) and KOH (15 g) were added to 500 mL
of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and stirred at 60◦Cf o r7d a y s ,
yieldingadeepred,transparentANFdispersion.Thedispersion
was then added to water to induce protonation and reduction,
followedbyhomogenizationtoobtainastablydispersedaramid
nanofibersuspension.
4.3 PreparationofHydrophilicCopperSheets
Polishedcoppersheetswereimmersedina15wt.%HNO 3 solution
for 5 min to remove the surface oxide layer. The etched copper
sheets were then treated in a 30 wt.% H2O2 solution for 60 min
toinduceoxidation.Theoxidizedcoppersheetswererinsedwith
deionizedwater,driedinanovenat60 ◦C,andfinallyannealed
inamufflefurnaceat300 ◦C.
4.4 PreparationofWettability-ConfinedSurfaces
Thelaserengravingmachineinline-scanmode(10W)wasused
to pattern FEP films with adhesive backing (thickness 120µm),
creating spaced FEP stripes by ablating the designated regions.
ThepatternedFEPstripeswerethenadheredtothehydrophilic
coppersubstratestoproducethewettability-confinedsurfaces.
4.5 PreparationofOrientedPorousAerogels
A square PTFE tubular mold was sealed onto the wettability-
confined surface. Aramid nanofibers (2 wt.%) and polyvinyl
alcohol (5 wt.%) were mixed at a 1:1 volume ratio to obtain a
dispersiondenotedasAP.TheAPsolutionwasuniformlymixed
withwaterbornepolyurethaneatvariousvolumefractions(30%,
40%, and 50%) to prepare the slurry. The mold containing the
slurrywasplacedinadirectionalfreezingsetupforfreezecasting.
After complete freezing, the samples were removed from the
mold and freeze-dried at−90 ◦C under 0.03 mbar for 48 h,
yieldingaerogelswithanorientedporousstructure.
4.6 CharacterizationandMeasurement
Themicrostructureoftheoxidized copper sheetswas examined
using a field-emission scanning electron microscope (Gem-
iniSEM 300, ZEISS, Germany). The microstructures of the
aerogels and wettability-confined surfaces were observed using
a super-depth three-dimensional optical microscope system
(VHX-6000, Keyence, Japan). The crystalline morphology of
the oxidized copper sheets was analyzed by X-ray diffraction
(MINFLEX600, Rigaku, Japan). Surface wettability was char-
acterized using a contact angle goniometer (DSA35, Kruss,
Germany).Tensilestress–straintestswereconductedonaerogel
(10 ×4 ×100mm)usinganelectronicuniversalmaterialtesting
machine(3367,Instron,USA)atatensilerateof10mm/min.
4.7 TestingofAerogelSensingProperties
One side of a commercial double-sided conductive tape was
adhered to an acrylic plate, and an aerogel (2.5× 3 × 0.5 cm)
wasattachedtotheothersideofthetape,servingasthepositive
triboelectric layer of the TENG. Similarly, a FEP film (25µm)
of the same area was fixed onto an acrylic plate using double-
sidedconductivetape,servingasthenegativetriboelectriclayer
oftheTENG.Thepositiveandnegativetriboelectriclayerswere
connected to a programmable electrometer (Keithley 6514) via
copperwires,andthetriboelectricperformancewastestedusing
alinearmotorinacontact–separationmode.
4.8 AssemblyofTriboelectricSensingArray
Theaerogelwascutintoeightrectangularblocks(10 ×20 ×2mm)
and assembled with patterned serpentine double-sided conduc-
tive tape. To ensure the stretchability of the sensing interface,
the aerogel and conductive tape were mounted on a highly
elasticpolyurethanefilmsubstrate(thickness:0.1mm).APDMS
film (40 × 150 × 0.5 mm) was placed above the aerogel as
the negative triboelectric material. The junction between the
polyurethane film and the PDMS film was encapsulated with
transparent VHB tape. The 8 TENG units were divided into
four groups(U1-U4), with the serpentine electrodes connected
to a multi-channel wireless sensing module. The triboelectric
signalsfromthetriboelectricsensingarraywererecordedinreal
time via Bluetooth using a computer-based reception software.
Additionally, in the vibration feedback module, the four sensor
groups were connected to linear vibration motors (rated 5 V,
60 mA) via TENG relay modules operating in different modes,
with the motors powered by CR2032 button cells. Distinct
vibration feedback was generated depending on the contact
positions.
4.9 OrientationAnalysis
The pore orientation of the aerogel was quantitatively analyzed
and compared using the Directionality plugin of ImageJ soft-
ware. This plugin calculates the structural contribution at each
anglebasedonFourierspectrumanalysis,generatesanorienta-
tiondistributionhistogram,andobtainsquantitativeorientation
parameters through Gaussian fitting. The angle range was set
from −90◦ to90◦ (100%indicatesperfectalignment,0%indicates
noalignment).
